A conversational website Montreal visitors will finish is not a floating bubble that says "Hi, how can I help?" It is a structured guide: a few questions, clear next steps, and a clean handoff into your CRM or calendar.

## A concrete lead-guide workflow

1. Pick one high-intent page (services, pricing intent, or contact).
2. Design a short decision tree: need, timeline, budget band, and fit checks.
3. Route qualified visitors to book a call or a human follow-up form.
4. Route poor fits to resources or a polite close. Do not fake a sales path.
5. Sync answers as CRM fields, not a blob note.

We build on tools like Voiceflow or Typebot, sometimes with a constrained model for free-text clarification. The site stays yours.

## Worked example: services triage

A consulting site collected "tell us everything" forms. Half the submissions were students or vendors. Sales spent mornings sorting.

We replaced the form on the services page with a five-step guide. Students hit a resources path. Vendors hit a partnerships email. Operator leads booked into Cal.com with the qualification fields already filled. Volume of meetings dropped slightly. Show-up quality rose because the wrong people self-selected out.

## Constraint and tradeoff

Long chats feel innovative and abandon at step eight. We keep paths short. If you need deep diagnosis, that is a call, not a widget.

Another tradeoff: putting the widget on every page creates noise. One or two high-intent surfaces outperform sitewide decoration.
