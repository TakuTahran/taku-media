AI implementation Montreal projects fail when the model works in a sandbox and dies in the Tuesday afternoon queue. We treat implementation as wiring: accounts, permissions, prompts or agents with owners, and a rollback path.

## A concrete implementation workflow

1. Freeze the pilot scope in writing (one workflow, one success metric, named owners).
2. Connect identity and data access with least privilege.
3. Build the happy path, then the failure path (timeouts, empty fields, bad model output).
4. Shadow mode for a defined window: AI drafts, humans send.
5. Cut over with an SOP and a person who can disable the flow without calling us at midnight.

We implement around third-party tools. We are not shipping a proprietary platform you must rent forever.

## Worked example: CRM notes to follow-up drafts

Sales logged calls inconsistently. Managers could not coach from empty notes.

We added a post-call step: transcript in, structured note fields out, optional follow-up email draft in the CRM. Reps edit before send. After two weeks of shadow mode, empty-note rate dropped because the default path was easier than skipping.

The hard part was field mapping and permission scopes, not the model. That is typical.

## Constraint and tradeoff

Shadow mode costs parallel effort. Skipping it to "move faster" usually means a public mistake and a trust reset that takes longer than the shadow period would have.

Vendor lock-in is another tradeoff. Deep custom work inside one SaaS is faster short term and painful when pricing or features shift. We document exit assumptions when we touch [workflow automation](/en/services/operations/workflow-automation) layers.
