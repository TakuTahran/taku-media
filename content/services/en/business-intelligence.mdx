AI business intelligence Montreal teams want is usually one sentence: answer the question without waiting on a analyst export. That only works when definitions are shared and the warehouse or warehouse-lite is trustworthy.

## A concrete BI workflow

1. Agree metric definitions in writing (revenue, active client, churn).
2. Connect a governed dataset, not every spreadsheet on the shared drive.
3. Add a question interface with row-level limits and logged queries.
4. Route ambiguous answers to a human analyst tag, not a fake confident number.
5. Publish a short catalog of approved questions so people reuse known-good prompts.

We implement on tools like Metabase or Power BI, sometimes with an LLM layer for phrasing. We do not invent a new analytics product.

## Worked example: "Which clients are quiet?"

Leadership asked weekly which retainers had gone dark. The answer lived in project tool last-activity dates plus unpaid invoice aging, previously stitched by hand.

We defined "quiet" as no billable activity in 14 days and no open ticket, then exposed a saved question. The LLM layer helps staff rephrase, but the metric stays fixed. When someone asks something undefined, the system says it cannot answer rather than improvising.

## Constraint and tradeoff

Natural language on dirty data produces confident nonsense. Cleaning and defining metrics is most of the work. If that cleanup is refused, we will recommend classic dashboards only.

Access control is the other tradeoff. Opening payroll-adjacent tables to a chat interface is a governance failure. We will block scopes rather than ship a demo that looks clever.
