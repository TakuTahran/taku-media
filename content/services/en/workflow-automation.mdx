Workflow automation Montreal work usually starts with a boring inventory: which systems hold customer truth, which ones get updated by hand, and which updates fail silently. We map those paths before writing a single automation.

## A concrete workflow we wire often

A typical first build looks like this:

1. New deal marked closed-won in the CRM.
2. Automation creates the client folder, invoice draft, and kickoff checklist.
3. Support tool gets a tagged contact so tickets do not start from a blank profile.
4. Failures post to a shared channel with the payload, not a vague "something failed" email.

We implement this on **n8n**, **Make**, or **Zapier** depending on hosting needs, budget, and who will maintain it. We do not sell an automation platform. We integrate the one that fits.

## Worked example: quote to onboarding

A services firm sent signed quotes by email, then retyped the same fields into billing and a project board. Two people spent part of every Monday reconciling mismatches.

We replaced the retyping with a single approved trigger: when the quote status flips to signed, the automation writes the project, copies line items into billing as a draft (not auto-sent), and opens three checklist tasks. Humans still approve the invoice. The automation removes transcription, not judgment.

## Constraint and tradeoff

Automations that skip approval on money or client-facing messages create worse problems than the manual work they remove. We bias toward draft-then-approve for anything financial or external.

Tool choice matters. Zapier is fastest for simple SaaS-to-SaaS links. Make handles branching affordably. n8n is better when you need self-hosting or complex transforms. Picking the wrong one locks you into brittle workarounds within months.
