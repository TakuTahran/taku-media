AI strategy Montreal conversations go wrong when they start with a vendor demo. We start with work that already hurts: slow handoffs, uneven quality, and data trapped in tools nobody queries.

## A concrete planning workflow

1. Inventory tools, owners, and the weekly rituals that keep the business moving.
2. Score candidate use cases on impact, data readiness, and risk if the model is wrong.
3. Pick one pilot with a measurable exit: time saved, error rate, or cycle time.
4. Write the stop conditions: what kills the pilot, who can override the tool, and where human review stays mandatory.

The output is a short sequence, not a 40-page deck. Usually three pages: priorities, constraints, and the first build.

## Worked example: support before marketing

A team wanted image generation for social first. Intake showed support tickets repeating the same five answers while seniors rewrote every reply.

We ranked support deflection higher because the knowledge already existed in past tickets, the risk of a wrong answer was containable with escalation rules, and the time cost was daily. Marketing content stayed on the list, but as phase two after [AI customer support](/en/services/customer-experience/ai-customer-support) patterns were stable.

## Constraint and tradeoff

Strategy without access to real systems is fiction. If we cannot see the CRM fields, ticket tags, or accounting categories, we will not invent a roadmap that pretends otherwise.

Another tradeoff: delaying tool purchases frustrates people who want momentum. Buying five overlapping subscriptions first is more expensive. We bias toward one paid stack per job until the pilot proves the job is real.
