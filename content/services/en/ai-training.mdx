AI training Montreal teams ask for is rarely "what is a large language model." They need habits: what to paste, what never to paste, how to review output, and when to stop.

## A concrete training workflow

1. Audit how people already use ChatGPT-class tools (including the unofficial ones).
2. Define allowed data classes and a short banned list.
3. Teach one job-shaped playbook per role (support reply, meeting summary, research brief).
4. Practice with live examples from your anonymized work, not generic demos.
5. Leave a one-page cheat sheet and an escalation path when the model is wrong.

Training without [AI implementation](/en/services/foundation/ai-implementation) still helps, but habits stick faster when the approved tool is already in the workflow.

## Worked example: bilingual support drafts

A support lead wanted faster replies in English and French. Staff were pasting full tickets into consumer chat tools, including customer addresses.

We ran a half-day session on redaction, tone checks, and a review checklist. Then we locked a workspace with approved prompts and logging. Speed improved. More important: address leakage stopped because the playbook made redaction the default first step.

## Constraint and tradeoff

One-off lunch-and-learns create a spike of curiosity and a quiet return to old habits. Durable change needs a named owner, a short refresh cadence, and consequences for unsafe use.

Another tradeoff: role-specific sessions cost more than one company-wide webinar. Generic sessions feel efficient and teach almost nothing transferable.
